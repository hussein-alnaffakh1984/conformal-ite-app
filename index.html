Intervals widen with Γ: a larger Γ allows stronger hidden confounding, so the worst-case weights move further from 1/e(x). An interval containing 0 means the sign of the effect is not established at this Γ.

MSM box vs moment-constrained worst case

Arm-1 cutoff for Γ = 1 … 8 on the current data. The moment constraint is the only difference between the two methods. The demo outcome (30-day readmission) is binary, so scores take few distinct values and widths move in steps; the gain here is small and irregular. The paper's continuous-outcome experiments give the comparison at matched guarantees.

Online monitoring: results from the paper

Not computed in your browser. One representative run of the paper's semi-synthetic stream (real covariates, simulated confounding with Γ* = 3); the numbers below each design are 20-run means.
fixed level (hidden under clipped ACI without drift) unclipped ACI (erodes the buffer) standard ACI, Γ = 1 clipped ACI (paper)
Top: factual coverage (treated units, observable). Bottom: counterfactual coverage (control units, observable only in simulation). Adapting the level on factual errors without clipping pulls factual coverage to 0.90 and removes the counterfactual buffer; clipping keeps it. After the mean shift, factual coverage stays high, so no factual-error rule reacts while counterfactual coverage falls.
Method: MSM box plus a moment constraint implied by the nominal propensity; exact worst case by a greedy rule (checked against the Charnes–Cooper LP). Same algorithm as the paper's Python code.
Simplifications in this demo: k-nearest-neighbour outcome model and a logistic propensity fitted to the recorded treatment on 50% of the rows, used without the error margin the theory requires (so intervals are illustrative); single split; no grid rounding of the test weight.
Demo data: Diabetes 130-US Hospitals (real covariates and recorded outcome (30-day readmission), 5,000-row random sample). Everything runs in your browser; no data leave this page.
